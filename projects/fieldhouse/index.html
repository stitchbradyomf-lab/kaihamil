How it works

Eight things we believe
Rule 01
Laptops are welcome

The whole point is that you don't have to pretend you're not working. Open it. Get something done. Nobody will judge you — they're doing the same thing.

Rule 02
No birthday parties. Ever.

They break the environment for everyone not invited. They create noise spikes, territorial seating, and the exact vibe we're here to escape. Not for any price.

Rule 03
Kids are supervised — not managed

Staff are present in all play zones. They're trained, attentive, and calm. But they're not running activities or blowing whistles. The play is self-directed.

Rule 04
The coffee is real

Single origin, dialed in, from a roaster we believe in. Not a Keurig in the corner. The coffee is a signal — it tells you what kind of place this is before you sit down.

Rule 05
No screens in the play zones

This is intentional. Parents at this price point are actively trying to give their kids physical, creative time. We protect that. Phones stay in the café.

Rule 06
Membership is the product

Recurring access means recurring investment — from us and from you. The space can be better because the economics are stable. No nickel-and-diming, ever.

Concept Document — Internal Brief

Overview The Insight The Model Real Estate Competitive Next Steps

What Fieldhouse is

A membership-based third place for professional parents and their children — combining co-working ergonomics, café quality, ambient child supervision, and physical play at a scale kids find legendary.

Not a trampoline park. Not a children's museum. Not a library. Not a gym. The best parts of each, without the worst of any.

The core insight

Every parent has sat next to the birthday cake, phone in hand, wishing they could open their laptop. The social permission to work while being present with your kids doesn't exist anywhere — not at Funzilla, not at a coffee shop, not at a playground.

Fieldhouse grants that permission by design. The environment itself makes it normal — even expected. Two guilt pressures (working on weekends, not engaging with kids) cancel each other out. The resolution is worth real money.

The operating model

Membership-only. No day passes, no pay-per-visit, no birthday party rentals. Recurring revenue enables investment in the space and creates a consistent, curated environment. 50 founding members at $179/month = $107K ARR before opening day.

All-inclusive. Coffee, play access, workspace — one price. Every micro-transaction is a small extraction. Every extraction builds quiet resentment. Fieldhouse charges once and delivers everything.

Waitlist as strategy. Limited founding memberships signal exclusivity, give pricing power, and let us tune member density before scaling. Demand outstripping supply is the best launch problem to have.

Real estate thesis

Target: 10,000–18,000 SF flex/industrial conversion within a 15-minute drive radius of Newtown Township, Yardley, and Langhorne — the highest-income family corridor in Bucks County (median HHI $131K–$166K).

Building requirements: high ceilings (16ft+), large open floor plate, strong parking ratio, loading access for equipment. Former big-box retail, sports facility, or light industrial preferred. Langhorne–Newtown–Warminster spine has active inventory at $13–$25/SF.

The indoor focus decouples us from traditional retail site selection. We don't need street visibility or foot traffic — we need drive-time proximity to affluent family zip codes.

Competitive whitespace

Funzilla (Langhorne) proves demand. It's built for kids; parents are an afterthought on plastic benches. Fieldhouse is Funzilla's demographic grown up.

No competitor has threaded: parent co-working + premium café + serious child play + membership model + no birthday parties. The whitespace is real and the market is demonstrably there.

Nearest next steps

1. Identify 3–5 candidate buildings along the Langhorne–Newtown corridor. Drive them. Photograph. Note ceilings, parking, neighbors.

2. Soft conversations with 5–10 parents in the target demo. Not a survey — real conversations. Does the concept land? What's missing?

3. Connect with one hospitality operator or commercial RE contact with experience converting flex space. Understand the permit and build-out reality.

4. Open the waitlist. Even a landing page with an email capture creates real signal. Demand before the building exists is the only validation that matters.
