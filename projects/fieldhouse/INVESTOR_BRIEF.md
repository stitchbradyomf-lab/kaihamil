# Fieldhouse — Investor Brief

**A membership space built for professional parents — where the work gets done and the kids don't want to leave.**

*Concept stage. Bucks County, PA. May 2026.*

---

## The One-Line Pitch

**The country club for the remote-work parent era.** A premium membership third place where parents can actually work — laptop open, real coffee, fast wifi — while their kids burn energy in legendary play zones in the same building. Membership-only, no birthday parties, all-inclusive pricing.

---

## The Problem

The post-2020 remote-work shift created a category of professional parents with three irreconcilable needs:

1. **Get focused work done** outside the home
2. **Be physically near their kids** on weekends and school breaks
3. **Not feel guilty** about either choice

Existing options force a choice:
- **Coffee shops** — hostile to kids, no play space
- **Coworking** — adult-only, expensive, sterile
- **Kids' play places** (Funzilla, trampoline parks) — sticky tables, plastic benches, no coffee program, no wifi, you're a chaperone not a person
- **Country clubs** — golf and tennis, not designed for working
- **Home** — the original problem

**Every parent has sat next to the birthday cake, phone in hand, wishing they could open their laptop — but knowing they couldn't.**

Fieldhouse is the place where they can.

---

## The Solution

A single ~12,000-15,000 SF premium membership space with three integrated zones:

**The Fieldwork** — Adult coworking café. Premium espresso program, sightlines to play zones, sound-managed open floor + private call booths, high-speed wifi.

**The Yard** — Legendary kids' play zone (ages 3-12). Signature climbing structure, age-zoned play, ambient supervision. The kind of place a 7-year-old tells their entire class about.

**The Workshop** — Maker space for parent-kid overlap. Light fabrication, drawing, design, seasonal projects. The inspiration half of "exhausted or inspired."

---

## The Model

**Membership-only.** No day passes. No pay-per-jump. No birthday party rentals.

| Tier | Price | Who it's for |
|------|-------|--------------|
| Solo | $129/mo | One adult, trade-off-with-partner families |
| Family | $229/mo | Two adults + up to three kids — core tier |
| Founding | $179/mo (locked) | First 50 members at permanent discount |

**Why membership:**
- Recurring revenue funds quality investment
- Curated community (member density managed)
- No nickel-and-diming creates the right vibe
- 50 founding members × $179 = $107K ARR locked before doors open

## Market Validation

**Demographic:** Bucks County professional families
- Target corridor: Newtown / Yardley / Langhorne
- Median household income: $131K-$166K
- High dual-income, remote-work, school-age children density

**Demand proof points:**
- Funzilla (Langhorne) operates at capacity weekends — proves play demand
- Bucks County coworking inventory at full occupancy — proves work demand
- Country clubs in the corridor have multi-year waitlists — proves membership willingness
- No competitor threads play + premium adult experience

**Whitespace:** Zero direct competitors. Adjacent operators serve one need or the other, never both.

## Real Estate Thesis

**Target building profile:**
- 10,000 - 18,000 SF
- 16+ ft ceilings (climbing structure)
- Strong parking ratio (3.5+ spaces per 1,000 SF)
- Loading access for equipment
- Flex / light industrial / former big-box retail
- Within 15-minute drive of Newtown / Yardley / Langhorne

**Why this works for CRE:**
- Long-term lease (10-year preferred) — stable tenant
- Low foot traffic dependency — indoor model decouples from retail visibility
- Tenant improvements paid by operator — landlord-friendly
- Strong demographic — affluent family corridor
- Active corridor inventory at $13-$25/SF NNN

**Building types of interest:**
- Former gyms / fitness clubs (existing high ceilings, parking)
- Light industrial conversions
- Underperforming big-box retail (Tuesday Morning, Pier 1 era vacancies)

## Why The Rules Matter (Moat)

**"No birthday parties ever"** is the strongest competitive moat in this category.

- Birthday parties break the environment for every non-invited member
- They create noise spikes, territorial seating, and the exact vibe parents pay to escape
- They generate $200K-$400K/year of "easy" revenue per location
- Competitors will not give that up

Fieldhouse sacrificing birthday revenue is the **signal of seriousness** that justifies the membership price. It's why the people who want this will pay for it, and why imitators won't keep up.

## Risk Assessment

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Capital intensity | Phased build, SBA leverage, founding member pre-revenue |
| Operational complexity | Hire experienced GM with hospitality + family entertainment background |
| Insurance / liability (kids + adults) | Specialty broker, age-gated zones, robust waivers |
| Membership churn (kids age out) | Workshop tier extends utility to ages 12-15; alumni member tier |
| Pandemic / closure risk | Membership freeze policy, hybrid digital community tier |
| Single-location concentration | Phase 2 expansion plan diversifies after Year 2 |

## What Fieldhouse Is Not

- It is not a daycare. Parents are present.
- It is not a kid's gym. There are no classes or trainers.
- It is not a coworking space. Kids are the whole point.
- It is not a country club. There's no golf, no tennis, no dress code.
- It is not a one-time-visit attraction. It's a recurring third place.

**Fieldhouse is the resolution of a tension every modern parent feels.** That tension is worth paying to resolve.
