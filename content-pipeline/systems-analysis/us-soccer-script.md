# US Soccer Systems Analysis — 60 Second Script

## Hook (0-5s)
"The US spends more on youth soccer than any country. So why do we keep failing? It's the system."

## Component 1: The Pay-to-Play Barrier (5-15s)
"First, the money flow. In Europe, elite academies are free — clubs invest in talent. In the US, parents pay three to ten thousand dollars a year for competitive clubs. We've created a unique paid middle tier that doesn't exist anywhere else."

## Component 2: The Talent Gap (15-25s)
"This filters out talent before discovery. The myth is 'if they're good enough, we'll find them.' But Eric Wynalda said it best: what if the moment he's found is also the moment his development is already damaged?"

## Component 3: Fragmented Governance (25-35s)
"Third, no one runs US soccer. USSF doesn't control the elite leagues — ECNL, Girls Academy, they're private. No unified philosophy, no coordinated pathway."

## Component 4: The NCAA Detour (35-45s)
"Then there's the college trap. Ages 18 to 22 — prime development years — spent in NCAA instead of pro. Europeans sign pro contracts at 16, 17. We have a four-year gap."

## Component 5: The Philosophy Divide (45-55s)
"Here's the core difference. In Europe, talent is a national resource. In the US, it's a private commodity. Until that changes, the system keeps eating its young."

## Close (55-60s)
"Systems determine outcomes. Chick-fil-A built a winning system. US Soccer built a filtering machine. Which system should I break down next?"

## Key Numbers to Remember
- $3,000-10,000+/year for US competitive clubs
- ~30 MLS academies (free but limited)
- Ages 18-22: NCAA gap vs European pro at 16-18
- Europe: 100% free academies, club-funded

## Contrast with Chick-fil-A
- Chick-fil-A: invests in human capital quality
- US Soccer: filters by income before assessing talent
- Both are systems — one designed for excellence, one designed for revenue

## Optional Add (90s version)
"The limited free pathways — MLS academies — are actually good. But there are only 30 of them. Vast geographic gaps. A kid in rural Texas or inner-city Detroit has no path unless parents can pay."
