# Video Library: Persona Credibility Series
## "As a [Persona], I had to..." Format

---

## Format Structure

**Opening:** "As a [Persona] at [Type of Organization], I had to..."

**The Challenge:** Specific, relatable situation (30-45 seconds)
- The messy reality
- The stakes
- The friction

**The Insight:** The pattern I discovered (15-20 seconds)
- Not just *what* I did, but *why* it mattered
- Connect to System//Self concept

**The Framework:** Universal principle (15-20 seconds)
- How this applies beyond my specific context
- Invitation to the viewer

**Closing:** Call to action or reflection (10 seconds)

**Total runtime:** ~90 seconds per video

## Persona Scripts

### 1. Data Product Owner

**Opening:**
"As a Data Product Owner at a leading financial information company, I had to ship a vendor quality platform that 40+ stakeholders agreed on... when they all wanted different things."

**The Challenge:**
"Every team had their own definition of 'quality.' Operations wanted speed. Compliance wanted perfection. Sales wanted flexibility. I was stuck in endless alignment meetings, each one creating more divergence. Six months in, we had PowerPoints but no product."

**The Insight:**
"I realized I wasn't managing a product—I was managing attention. Everyone's cognitive capacity was fragmented across competing priorities. The real job wasn't building consensus. It was creating a single source of truth that made the trade-offs visible."

**The Framework:**
"This is what I now call the Communication Layer—the first step in any System transformation. You can't automate what you can't see. You can't optimize what you haven't named."

**Closing:**
"Where is your team spending attention without visibility? That's your starting point."

---

### 2. Technology Project Manager

**Opening:**
"As a Technology Project Manager, I had to migrate a legacy data pipeline to the cloud... with zero downtime, 99.5% accuracy requirements, and a team that had never done infrastructure work."

**The Challenge:**
"The technical risk was manageable. The human risk wasn't. Engineers were terrified of breaking something that touched $40 billion in daily transactions. Every decision required three levels of approval. Progress moved at the speed of fear."

**The Insight:**
"I stopped managing the project and started architecting confidence. We built a 'shadow system' that ran parallel—proving the new pipeline worked before we touched production. The technical achievement was secondary. The psychological safety was primary."

**The Framework:**
"This is the Infrastructure-First approach. System before Self. Build the container before you pour the liquid. Most projects fail not because the work is hard, but because the confidence isn't structured."

**Closing:**
"What are you trying to pour before you've built the container?"

---

### 3. Workflow Developer

**Opening:**
"As a Workflow Developer, I had to automate vendor operations that had been done manually for 15 years... without the vendors realizing anything changed."

**The Challenge:**
"The existing process was held together by tribal knowledge. One person knew how to handle exceptions. Another knew the client-specific rules. Documentation was folklore. If I automated the documented process, I'd break everything. If I interviewed everyone, I'd never ship."

**The Insight:**
"I started with observation, not automation. I watched the work for two weeks. What I found: 60% of the 'process' was actually workarounds for upstream problems. Automating bad process just makes bad output faster."

**The Framework:**
"This is Life Process Mapping—understanding the actual flow before you optimize it. Most workflow developers skip this step. They build what people *say* they do instead of what they *actually* do. The gap is where your automation breaks."

**Closing:**
"Before you automate, are you mapping the real process or the assumed one?"

---

### 4. Quality Analyst

**Opening:**
"As a Quality Analyst, I had to improve data accuracy from 92% to 99.5%... across 12 different data sources, each with their own definition of 'correct.'"

**The Challenge:**
"Every source owner swore their data was clean. But when we cross-referenced, we found 40% of 'quality issues' were actually integration mismatches—different timestamps, different units, different null handling. The problem wasn't quality control. It was quality translation."

**The Insight:**
"I stopped measuring accuracy and started measuring alignment. We built a 'common cause variation' dashboard that showed where discrepancies came from—source issues, transformation issues, or timing issues. For the first time, we could see the real problem."

**The Framework:**
"This is Common Cause vs. Special Cause analysis—understanding whether your problem is systemic or situational. Most quality efforts fail because they treat symptoms (special causes) when the disease is structural (common causes)."

**Closing:**
"Are you fixing the glitch or the system that produces glitches?"

---

### 5. Intelligence Analyst

**Opening:**
"As an Intelligence Analyst, I had to deliver actionable insights to executives... from incomplete data, under time pressure, with high-stakes decisions hanging on my analysis."

**The Challenge:**
"The data was ambiguous. The timeline was impossible. The executives wanted certainty. I found myself either oversimplifying—giving confident answers I knew were incomplete—or overqualifying—burying insights in caveats and confidence intervals. Neither served the decision."

**The Insight:**
"I started framing my output as 'decision support' rather than 'analysis.' Instead of 'the data shows,' I said 'given what we know, the range of reasonable outcomes is...' I gave them the uncertainty, structured. They could make the call with eyes open."

**The Framework:**
"This is the Uncertainty Framework—moving from 'what do I know' to 'what do I know about what I don't know.' Intelligence isn't about being right. It's about being calibrated. Decision-makers don't need certainty. They need clarity on the uncertainty."

**Closing:**
"When you don't have perfect information, do you hide the gaps or illuminate them?"

---

### 6. People Leader

**Opening:**
"As a People Leader, I had to scale a team from 5 to 25 people... without losing the culture, burning out the veterans, or drowning myself in management overhead."

**The Challenge:**
"Every new hire changed the chemistry. Every process I documented created rigidity. Every meeting I added reduced output. I was becoming a bottleneck—every decision flowed through me. The team was growing, but our effectiveness was shrinking."

**The Insight:**
"I shifted from 'managing people' to 'designing systems that manage the work.' Instead of checking work, I built checklists. Instead of resolving conflicts, I created decision frameworks. Instead of being the source of truth, I built dashboards. My job became building the machine, not running it."

**The Framework:**
"This is the System//Self balance for leaders. Your Self work—coaching, vision, culture—requires that your System work—process, structure, automation—is handled. You can't be present for your people if you're drowning in operational noise."

**Closing:**
"Are you leading people or managing the work they do? The difference is burnout."

## Recording Guidelines

### Visual Setup
- **Camera:** Eye level, 6 feet away, torso-up framing
- **Background:** Neutral office or soft home setting
- **Lighting:** Window or softbox from front-left
- **Audio:** Lapel mic or phone voice memo held close

### Delivery Style
- **Tone:** Conversational, not presentational
- **Pacing:** Pause after the challenge. Let it land.
- **Eye contact:** Camera lens, not screen
- **Energy:** Highest on the insight/framework transition

### Production
- **Raw footage:** 3-5 minutes per script
- **Edit to:** 90 seconds
- **Captions:** Required (most viewers watch silent)
- **Thumbnail:** Face + text overlay: "As a [Persona]..."

### Distribution
- **LinkedIn:** Primary platform (professional credibility)
- **YouTube Shorts:** Secondary (discovery)
- **Kai Hamil site:** Embed in framework pages
- **Email:** Include transcript for readers
