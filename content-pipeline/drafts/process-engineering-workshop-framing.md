# Process Engineering Workshop Framing

*Designing workflows that serve purpose, not space*

---

## The Hook

Unloading the dishwasher. Carrying glasses from Miele dishwasher to soft-close cabinets. Realizing: **the kitchen design is wrong.**

We are acting *because of* the space, instead of having the space act *for us*.

This is the distinction. This is process engineering.

---

## What Process Engineering Is

Not woo-woo intentionality. Technical, rigorous, grounded.

**The questions:**
- What are your constraints?
- What can you control?
- Where will you see variability?

**The philosophy:**
Design according to purpose, not according to how it currently works.

---

## The Core Principle

A well-designed process emerges from the **best path from where you are to where you need to go**.

Synonymous with:
- Define your inputs
- Define your outputs
- The process engineering becomes feasible

Not simple. Feasible.

---

## Critical Constraints

Good process engineering depends on:

1. **What am I getting?** (inputs)
2. **What do I need to produce?** (outputs)

With surrounding context:
- Scientific validation
- Measurement, counting, metrics
- Health scores

**The goal:** Not perfect dashboards. A measurement system that defines **what is done and what is good**.

---

## Success Metrics

**How many things do you get right?**
**How many things do you get done?**

In that order.

Getting things done without getting them right:
- Partly done
- Negatively done (reducing value)
- Erroneous or incomplete output

---

## System Thinking

Process engineering applies to any system with:
- Series of inputs and outputs
- Governed not by central definition of good
- But by **relationship to other processes in the system**

---

## The Kitchen Example

Unloading dishes is not the point.

The point: **What should a kitchen provide?**

Think of it as a product:
- What value does it add?
- What function does it provide?
- What does it do for me that reduces my burden?

**Important caveat:** A kitchen is a distinctly human space. The act of cooking is not to be automated away.

## Theory of Constraints

Each system is only as good as its **primary bottleneck**.

The most narrow part of the pipe. Where stuff gets backed up.

The whole system is only as good as that limitation.

Making other things faster—investing in other aspects—creates **local optimization**.

Local optimization is dangerous because:
- It sounds good
- You can report on it
- You can measure success with data: "Look how much better I am"

But the gotcha: **What happened to the customer?** What happened next? Did this actually reduce cost? Ship more product? Produce more value?

"No, but our efficiency is 30% higher."

**Efficiency for what?** Moving from point C to point D?

Great. The bottleneck was at point F. You just get to the bottleneck faster. The bottleneck fills up even greater. Then the bottleneck is blamed.

Yes, the bottleneck is the constraint—but you invested in the wrong place.

---

## Not All Bottlenecks Are Equal

Some bottlenecks, if addressed, open a floodgate of improvement.

Others just yield the next constraint. And the next. Indefinitely—because there's a structural problem.

## Metrics and the Target

Metrics and KPIs are only as good as their **predictive and representative relationship to the bottleneck** in the way of hitting your target.

And in order for there to be a bottleneck, **there needs to be a target**.

Otherwise the concept of speed and throughput do not matter.

Which might be the case in some processes—but certainly not in anything you're paying for.

---

## The Implication

Without a target:
- Speed is meaningless
- Throughput is irrelevant
- Optimization is theater

With a target:
- The bottleneck reveals itself
- Metrics become meaningful
- Process engineering has purpose

**The sequence:**
1. Define the target
2. Identify the bottleneck
3. Engineer the process
4. Measure against constraint

Not the reverse.

## Vendor Operations = People Operations

**Vendor versus internal employee is a lower-level distinction.**

It doesn't apply to the automation space.

**The real filter:** People versus machine.

**The hierarchy of substitutes:**
- What needs to be done?
- What are the possible paths?
- Which path is better?
- Follow that path.

Sometimes the path leads to a vendor.  
Sometimes it leads elsewhere.

**The trend:** Paths to vendors are decreasing. Paths to automation are increasing.

This is the shift. This is the work.

## Human-in-the-Loop Process Engineering

**Anchor on where people need to be involved.**

Then optimize everything else around it.

### The Four Levels

| Level | Human Role | AI Role | Classification |
|-------|------------|---------|----------------|
| **1. Full Control** | Human primary | None | Human primary |
| **2. AI Assisted** | Human decides | AI supports | Human primary |
| **3. AI Performed, Human Approves** | Human validates | AI executes | AI primary |
| **4. AI Performed, Human Aware** | Human notified | AI executes | AI primary |

**The split:**
- Top two levels: Human primary
- Bottom two levels: AI primary

### The Design Sequence

1. Identify what needs human involvement (Levels 1-2)
2. Protect and optimize those areas
3. Automate everything else (Levels 3-4)
4. Measure against the bottleneck

This is the framework. This is the work.
