# I Love to Love

I love to love.

That might sound obvious, but saying it out loud matters. Because being in a loving relationship is one of the most magical things a human can experience — and it absolutely *sucks* when life gets in the way.

## The Growing Weight

When my wife and I first got together, love was easy. It was late nights, shared jokes, the feeling of being seen. But as our family grew — three kids, two careers, a house, aging parents — the shape of love changed. And I didn't notice until it was almost too late.

My wife's needs evolved. What she needed at 28 isn't what she needs at 40. The woman I married wanted adventure and spontaneity. The woman I'm married to now wants presence and partnership. Both are valid. Both deserve to be met. But I was still operating on old software, and she was running new code, and neither of us realized we were speaking different languages until the connection started fraying.

Being responsible for yourself is hard enough. Being responsible for yourself *and* a relationship? That's stewardship. That's a job. That's work that most people don't sign up for explicitly — they just wake up one day and realize they're managing a small corporation with someone they used to have fun with.

## When Love Becomes Logistics

Here's what I didn't understand for years: any change in tension affects the other side immediately.

When I'm stressed about work, I get quiet. When I get quiet, she feels abandoned. When she feels abandoned, she gets anxious. When she gets anxious, I feel pressured. When I feel pressured, I get quieter. The loop feeds itself.

What once was love toward another person slowly, imperceptibly turned into shared responsibilities. Calendar coordination. Who's picking up whom. Did you call the plumber. The mental load of family life.

And here's the tragedy: that work is hardly romantic and easily dismissed. "I'm handling the logistics" feels like contribution. It feels like effort toward the relationship. But it's not effort toward *her*. She doesn't receive it as love. She receives it as... management. And management without connection is just administration.

I was working hard on our relationship while she felt increasingly alone in it. I was confusing effort toward shared responsibilities as effort toward my partner — who wasn't even receiving that effort directly.

## The Bilateral Trap

Fifty percent of marriages fail. Not because people stop loving each other. Not because they're incompatible. But because the bilateral burden becomes unbearable.

When two people own a relationship jointly, every misalignment becomes personal. Every dropped ball becomes blame. Every unmet expectation becomes a wound. The responsibility is shared, which means when things go wrong, there's no clear owner — only mutual disappointment.

This is why administration destroys relationships. Not because couples can't handle tasks. But because tasks that fall through the cracks have no owner. And blame without ownership erodes the very connection that makes the relationship worth maintaining.

## The Third Point

Three is stronger than two.

In game theory, the triangle is the strongest structure. A couple bears the responsibility of owning both themselves as individuals AND the relationship itself. That's two entities trying to manage three domains (you, me, us).

By adding a neutral third party — an objective system — stability is added. Problems within the relationship don't require adjudication. They require system tuning.

AI presents a new opportunity. A third party to shift responsibilities and expectations to, rather than blaming each other. A third entity triples the number of communication streams, easily allowing the two people to remain locked in and giving to each other — not confusing effort toward shared responsibilities as effort toward their partner, who doesn't even receive that effort directly.

When something falls through the cracks, it's not "you forgot" or "I failed." It's "the system needs tuning." The blame shifts to infrastructure. The grace returns to the relationship.

## Building Couples Valé

I'm building Couples Valé as the couples operating system — an AI approach to the challenges of daily and family life for a couple.

This isn't a shared to-do list. It's not a way to monitor your partner. It's not another app to check.

It's relationship infrastructure that preserves magic. A neutral third party for blame. Space to be human together. Relief from bilateral burden.

Couples Valé brings everything together, objectifies shared resources and responsibilities, and makes it possible for both people to "manage" their lives with AI — and "live" their lives with each other.

## Why This Matters

I'm doing this because it's the most important thing in the world to me. I see the impact that relationship strength has on my personal life. I am responsible for both things — myself, and my relationship.

I'm doing this not because I'm dependent on the relationship, but because I love to love. Being in a loving relationship is magical, and it absolutely sucks when life and administration get in the way.

When the burden of managing and optimizing not just yourself, but the relationship connection at the same time, becomes too heavy — that's where Couples Valé steps in.

It holds the administrative load so you can hold each other.
