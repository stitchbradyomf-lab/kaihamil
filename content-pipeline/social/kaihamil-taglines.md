# Kai Hamil Taglines by Platform

*Platform-native positioning for multi-channel presence*

---

## Primary Positioning

**Core:** System development for the agentic era
**Voice:** Clear, practical, quietly ambitious
**Differentiator:** Real failures as curriculum, not polished success stories

---

## Platform Taglines

### Substack — "Storytelling about systems"

**Why this works:**
- Substack rewards narrative depth
- Long-form essays need stories that carry ideas
- "Systems" signals intellectual weight without jargon
- Appeals to readers who want to think, not just execute

**Content type:**
- Case studies (Casey Neistat, Fieldhouse, failed cron jobs)
- Personal essays on method development
- Worked examples as stories

**Tone:** Reflective, analytical, occasionally self-deprecating

---

### LinkedIn — "Learning about intelligence"

**Why this works:**
- LinkedIn audience is professionally motivated
- "Intelligence" has double meaning (AI + human capability)
- Positions you as exploring, not claiming expertise
- Invites professional connection and inquiry

**Content type:**
- Framework posts (the Kai Hamil method)
- Professional development insights
- "What I'm learning" updates
- Course and workshop announcements

**Tone:** Professional, curious, generous with process

---

### YouTube — "Pursuing equilibrium"

**Why this works:**
- YouTube rewards visual narrative and personality
- "Equilibrium" is cinematic, visual, relatable
- Suggests journey, not destination
- Opens space for lifestyle + work integration content

**Content type:**
- Day-in-the-life with systems visible
- Build logs and behind-the-scenes
- Method explanations with visual demonstration
- Longer-form case studies

**Tone:** Present, observational, occasionally humorous

---

## Short-Form Layer: Intentional Dad

**Platforms:** Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok

**Character:** Framework-obsessed dad who applies work systems to family life
- Daily standups with toddlers
- OKRs for household chores
- Retrospectives on family vacations
- Gantt charts for birthday parties

**Why this works:**
- Self-deprecating humor lowers resistance
- Demonstrates method in absurd contexts
- Satire of optimization culture while participating in it
- Entry point to main content

**Tone:** Deadpan, committed to the bit, occasionally breaks character

**Example hooks:**
- "We ran a retro on our family vacation. The kids gave it a 3/10. Here's what we learned."
- "I implemented a kanban board for my daughter's morning routine. Velocity is down but predictability is up."
- "My wife asked me to 'just be present' at dinner. I explained that presence is a lagging indicator of system design."
