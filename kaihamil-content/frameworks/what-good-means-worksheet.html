What Good Means A worksheet for defining the marriage you actually want — with one small catch.

Before you can measure a gap, close a gap, or complain about a gap, you have to define the standard. This worksheet is where you write it down. Each spouse fills one out on their own. Then you trade.

Working assumption baked in: a marriage exists to increase joy, reduce burden, and share responsibility — ideally in that order.

Who's holding the pen?

⚠ THE ONLY RULE — READ BEFORE WRITING

Whatever you write down as an expectation of your spouse is, from this moment, expected of you too.

We call it the Mirror Rule, and it's doing quiet, important work: it makes "unload the dishwasher by 8pm" a very expensive thing to write, and "help that shows up before I ask" a very wise one. Tasks are outputs. Good is a standard. Write standards you'd be proud to be held to — because you're about to be.

SEC. 1

The Order of Operations

Three purposes, deliberately ranked. You'll write your definition of good under each one.

1

Increase joy

The point of the whole enterprise. If the marriage runs flawlessly and nobody's having any fun, congratulations — you've built a very efficient sadness.

2

Reduce burden

Life is heavy. A marriage should make each life in it lighter — not by keeping score, but because watching your person struggle and doing nothing isn't love.

3

Share responsibility

The mechanics. Two owners, no supervisor. Ranked third on purpose: it serves the first two. It is not the mission statement.

    WHAT THE MIRROR NOTICED

    These are observations about your story, not facts about your marriage. The most useful question isn't "who's right?" — it's "what would my partner's worksheet say?" Trade and find out.

    Define good once. Live it twice — once in what you hope for, once in what you give.

    North star, as always: an equal partner who shares the burden, the responsibility, and the joy — across a long and winding life together. When you've both finished, trade worksheets. The overlap is your marriage's definition of good. The differences are your next great conversation.
