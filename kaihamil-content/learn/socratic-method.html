Socratic Method for Kai Hamil

A learner-centered approach to teaching system development

The Core Shift

From: "Here's what I built and how you can use it"

To: "What do you need to build, and how can I help you discover it?"

The Socratic method fits your method because it puts the learner's needs first — not your examples, not your frameworks, but their actual situation and capacity.

Key Principles

Maieutics (midwifery)

What it means: Draw out what the learner already knows

How you apply it: They have ideas. Your job is to surface them, not supply them.

Elenchus (testing)

What it means: Examine beliefs through questioning

How you apply it: "What would have to be true for that to work?"

Aporia (productive confusion)

What it means: Create space where old answers don't fit

How you apply it: "If that's the solution, why hasn't it worked yet?"

Logos over authority

What it means: Reason matters more than your status

How you apply it: You're a guide, not a guru.

Six Question Types for Your Context

1. Clarification

"What do you mean by 'system' in your work?"

Force precision. Most people use words they haven't defined.

2. Assumption Probing

"What are you assuming about how your work should flow?"

Surface the invisible constraints they don't know they're operating under.

3. Evidence Examination

"How do you know that's the bottleneck?"

Separate intuition from data. Both matter, but know which you're using.

4. Viewpoint Exploration

"How would your [client/partner/employee] describe this same problem?"

Break the solipsism. Systems involve multiple actors.

5. Implication Tracing

"If you automate that decision, what happens to your attention?"

Every system change has second-order effects. Surface them early.

6. Meta-Reflection

"Why is that the most important question right now?"

Teach them to manage their own inquiry, not just answer yours.

The Socratic Arc in a Single Session

Opening (5 min): Clarify their immediate situation

  • "What brought you here today?"
  • "What have you already tried?"

Exploration (20 min): Probe assumptions and surface constraints

  • "What would have to change for that to work?"
  • "What's the real cost of doing nothing?"

Discovery (15 min): Draw out their own insight

  • "You mentioned X and Y — what's the connection you're seeing?"
  • "If you had to explain this to someone else, what would you say?"

Commitment (10 min): Define the smallest next step

  • "What's the first thing you could do to test this?"
  • "What would you need to know to feel confident starting?"

Closing (5 min): Solidify and schedule follow-up

  • "What surprised you in this conversation?"
  • "What will you do by [specific date]?"

How This Differs from Traditional Teaching

Traditional Socratic
"Here's the framework" "What framework do you need?"
"Let me show you my example" "What's your example?"
"This is how you apply it" "How would you apply this?"
Knowledge transfer Capacity building
Instructor-centered Learner-centered

Your Role as Socratic Guide

You are not:

  • The expert with answers
  • The validator of their ideas
  • The source of frameworks they must adopt

You are:

  • The mirror that reflects their thinking back to them
  • The questioner who surfaces what they haven't examined
  • The midwife who helps them birth their own insight

Your preparation matters more than your presentation:

  • Know the territory (your method, common patterns, failure modes)
  • Have worked examples ready (but don't force them)
  • Be prepared to be wrong about what they need

Application to Your Workshop

Week 1: Their Current State

  • Where are you losing time, attention, or energy?
  • What have you already tried to fix it?
  • What's the real constraint — time, knowledge, tools, or clarity?

Week 2: Their Ideal State

  • If this worked perfectly, what would be different?
  • Who else would notice? What would they see?
  • What's the smallest version of this that would matter?

Week 3: Their Path

  • What would you need to know to start?
  • What could you do in the next 48 hours?
  • What would tell you you're on the right track?

Week 4: Their System

  • What have you discovered that surprised you?
  • What will you actually implement?
  • What support do you need to sustain this?

The Risk and Reward

The Risk

Socratic teaching is slower. It requires patience. The learner might discover something you didn't anticipate — or fail to discover what you think is obvious.

The Reward

They own what they build. It fits their actual life, not your generic template. They can sustain it because they designed it.

Summary

The Socratic method applied to your work means:

  1. Start with their need, not your solution
  2. Ask questions that surface their own insight
  3. Test assumptions rather than supply answers
  4. Let them build the system that fits their life
  5. Your frameworks are tools they can choose, not truths they must accept

You're not teaching them to use your method. You're teaching them to develop their own.
