SEC. 2

Care & Feeding Instructions

Fourteen field-tested moves, organized by subsystem. Each one comes with a Field Test you can run tonight.

Ch. 1 How You Talk to Him

Ask about his problems, not his progress +

“Did you handle that thing yet?” is a status update. “What's actually weighing on you lately?” is a marriage. Most men aren't hiding their inner life — nobody's asked about it since 2019.

Field test: Ask one question tonight with zero logistical value. Then ask a follow-up. Watch what happens.
Requests in, work orders out +

“Can you grab this?” + a real thank-you lands completely differently than an assignment + irritation at the five-minute delay. Same task. One builds goodwill; the other builds a guy who technically complies.

Field test: One ask, one specific thanks. No inspection of the results.
Listen without issuing a verdict +

When he floats an idea, resist the instant performance review. Try “tell me more” before “here's what you're missing.” A man who gets graded every time he talks eventually stops talking — and then you'll miss the talking.

Field test: Next opinion he shares, respond with curiosity only. Verdicts are closed for the evening.
Say it straight — no hints, no tests +

“He should just know” is a pop quiz he didn't get the study guide for. He fails; you collect evidence he doesn't care; everybody loses. Being genuinely known beats being guessed at correctly, every time.

Field test: Want something this week? Say it in one plain sentence. Radical, we know.
Fight the problem, not the man +

“The trash didn't go out” gets fixed in a day. “You're so lazy” gets remembered for a decade. And mind the leaks — eye-rolls and heavy sighs are contempt in gesture form, and contempt is the termite of marriage.

Field test: One full disagreement, character insults off the table. Sighs count.

Ch. 2 How You Treat What He Does

His way is a way +

The towels are folded weird. The kids' outfits clash. The route to the restaurant was creative. Let. It. Ride. Every correction teaches him that trying isn't worth it — and then you're doing everything, resentful, married to a man you trained to stand down.

Field test: One task, his method, zero commentary. Bite the inside of your cheek if needed.
Praise with GPS coordinates +

“Thanks for handling the insurance call — I didn't have to think about it once” beats a generic “thanks” tenfold. Specific appreciation says I see you. Men will quietly move mountains for someone who notices.

Field test: Thank him for one thing so specifically he could find it on a map.
Brag publicly, gripe never +

Praising him to your friends, family, and kids is rocket fuel. Criticizing him in public — even “jokingly” — is a wound he won't mention and won't forget. If your group chat only knows his flaws, audit the story you're telling yourself too.

Field test: Brag about him to someone this week. Bonus points if he's in earshot.
Ask his advice — then actually take it sometimes +

Bring him a real decision and let his input genuinely change your course. Nothing says “I respect you” like his judgment mattering. Nothing says “you're decorative” like input you collect and shred.

Field test: One real question, one real consideration. Not a formality — a consultation.

Ch. 3 How You Stay Connected

Date your friend +

You were friends before you were co-managers of a household. Plan something with zero logistical purpose: a game night, a dumb competition, a show that's only yours. Shared fun isn't the bonus feature of a marriage — it's the operating system.

Field test: Put one purely-fun thing on the calendar. No errands may attach themselves to it.
Drive-by affection +

A hand on his back. A shoulder squeeze on your way to the kitchen. Touch that asks for nothing and leads to nothing says you're wanted just for existing in my space — and for most men, that lands deeper than words.

Field test: Three casual touches today. No agenda permitted.
Make the first move +

In long marriages, desire is often responsive — it shows up after you begin, not before. If he's always the one reaching for you, he'll eventually stop reaching; not because he stopped wanting you, but because losing bets get abandoned. You initiating, sometimes, says: I still choose you.

Field test: You know exactly what this one is.
Show him the unarmored version +

Let him see you uncertain or scared — not as a strategy, and without grading his response afterward. Vulnerability without a scorecard says I trust you. If he only ever meets your armor, he can only ever love your armor.

Field test: Share one real worry this week. Expect nothing back. See what comes anyway.
Guard his garage time +

The friend group, the hobby, the twenty minutes of silence after work — protect it instead of resenting it. He comes back from those things a better husband. Be the person who restores him, not the last demand of his day.

Field test: Next time he heads to his thing, send him off warmly. Watch him come back different.
SEC. 3

Diagnostic: Boss or Teammate?

Five questions. Honesty setting: maximum. Nobody's watching.

When he finishes a chore, I usually re-do or “fix” part of it.

I can't remember the last time I asked how he's actually doing.

I tell him what to do more often than I ask him.

My friends have heard more complaints about him than brags.

He initiates affection way more than I do.

SEC. 4

This Week's Missions

Pick a few. Small moves, run consistently, change everything.

0 of 7 — the manual is judging you gently.

He's not your employee, your project, or your report card. He's the man you chose — keep choosing him out loud.

The mirror test: if someone ran you the way you run him — the tone, the corrections, the scorekeeping — would you feel loved, or managed? Manage the household together. Love the man. Different jobs. Only one keeps him close.
