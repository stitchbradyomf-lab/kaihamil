## Cadence & Production Loop

### Publishing cadence: **One video every 7 days**

Why not more:
- Medium-form takes real production time (writing, shooting, editing, thumbnail, metadata)
- Sustainable for someone with a full-time job, a family, and the rest of the KH stack
- Beats the algorithm's "consistency" check (1/week is consistent enough)
- Leaves bandwidth for Tier 1 creation in other formats (Life & Times, films later)

Why not less:
- Channels that publish less than weekly lose audience compounding
- Anything slower invites the "is this channel alive?" question
- Hard to build cross-references between videos with a >7 day gap

### Production loop (7-day cycle)

| Day | Activity | Time investment |
|-----|----------|----------------|
| Sun | Script the next video (using outlines from queue) | 60-90 min |
| Mon | Shoot A-roll (talking head) | 60-90 min |
| Tue | Stitch helps pull B-roll, queue thumbnail concepts | 30-60 min |
| Wed | Edit primary cut | 90-120 min |
| Thu | Edit polish + thumbnail finalize | 60-90 min |
| Fri | Metadata, scheduling, cross-references | 30 min |
| **Sat or Sun** | **Publish** | — |

**Total: ~6-8 hours per video.** That's the budget. If a video doesn't fit, it isn't ready.

### When to break the cadence
- If a video isn't strong enough, **skip the week**. One excellent video > four mediocre ones.
- If a Tier 1 opportunity outside YouTube emerges (a film, a Life & Times edition), let YouTube wait one cycle.
- Never break cadence for fear of perfection — break it only for genuine quality or genuine alternative output.

## Substack & LinkedIn Co-Benefit Strategy

These two are NOT replacement formats. They are **distribution layers and conversation extensions** that ride off the YouTube core.

### Substack
- Publish a written essay version of every YouTube video
- Essay can be **the script itself, polished as prose** (zero additional research)
- Substack audience tends to be thoughtful, can convert to email subscribers, supports Tier 3 funnels (workshop, products)
- Cadence: same as YouTube (1 essay = 1 video)
- Long-term: Substack becomes the canonical written archive of the channel

### LinkedIn
- Publish a **single-paragraph distillation** of each video the day after publish
- Format: hook → key insight → "more in the video" link
- LinkedIn audience tends to be operators/leaders who don't have 10 minutes for a video but might
- Cadence: same as YouTube (1 post = 1 video, time-shifted)
- Long-term: LinkedIn is the bridge to the operator/consulting audience

### Critical rule
- **Both must reference the YouTube video as the canonical source.**
- Never let Substack or LinkedIn become the primary publication
- The YouTube video is the artifact. Everything else is its echo.

## Failure Modes To Watch For

### The early-discouragement spiral
First 3 videos will get small numbers. They always do. **This is information about the algorithm, not about the work.** Stitch will reframe analytics conversations during this period: low views ≠ low quality.

### The pivot temptation
Around video 4-6, there's a natural "should I change the format?" moment. **Don't.** The architecture is sound. Trust the queue.

### The shorts-first slip
If Kyle is tempted to shift focus to shorts because they get more views, Stitch will remind him: shorts are distribution residue, not the work. The medium-form is the channel.

### The comparison trap
Other channels grow faster. Some grow viral overnight. **The expertise lane is slower, deeper, and more durable.** Stitch will surface comparable expertise-channel growth curves (Doctor Alex took months) so Kyle has the right reference set.

### The "should this be a Substack post instead" mistake
Once Substack is co-publishing, there will be a temptation to "save the bandwidth and just write." Don't. **The video is the artifact. The essay is the echo.**
