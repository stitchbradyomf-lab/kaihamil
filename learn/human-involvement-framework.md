# Human Involvement Framework

*Categorizing time and focus by depth of participation*

---

## Overview

A framework for designing business processes and personal systems based on how deeply a human should be involved. Not what *can* be automated—but what level of human presence serves the purpose.

The resource being allocated: **time and focus** (tracked, finite, precious).

---

## The Four Levels

### Level 1: Full Control
**Depth:** Deep focus, full presence  
**Human role:** The human *is* the process  
**Function:** Identity-forming, meaning-making

**When to use:**
- The activity shapes who you are
- Presence itself is the value
- Automation would destroy the purpose

**Examples:**
- Reading a story to a child at bedtime
- Having a sensitive conversation with a partner
- Writing a letter by hand
- Delivering an investor pitch
- Negotiating a merger
- Firing someone with dignity

**Design principle:** Protect ferociously. These are not efficiency opportunities.

---

### Level 2: Heavy Involvement
**Depth:** Significant input, final decision  
**Human role:** Decides, may use AI advice  
**Function:** Values-expressing, judgment-applying

**When to use:**
- The outcome reflects your values
- Stakes are high but not existential
- You want ownership of the result

**Examples:**
- Choosing where to live, which house to buy
- Designing bedroom furniture layout
- Planning a family trip
- Product strategy decisions
- Hiring key roles
- Setting pricing models
- Defining brand voice

**Design principle:** AI supports, human decides. The tool serves the judgment.

---

### Level 3: Approval
**Depth:** Review and confirm  
**Human role:** Validates, approves, occasionally adjusts  
**Function:** Judgment-applying at scale, exception-handling

**When to use:**
- Pattern is established, exceptions matter
- Speed helps, blind trust hurts
- Human taste or ethics should gate

**Examples:**
- Approving weekly meal plan
- Allocating budget to new services
- Vendor selection from shortlist
- Marketing spend authorization
- Content calendar confirmation
- Customer refund decisions

**Design principle:** AI proposes, human disposes. Default to yes, flag for no.

---

### Level 4: Awareness
**Depth:** Notification, no action required  
**Human role:** Knows it happened, maintains context  
**Function:** Context-maintaining, anomaly-detection

**When to use:**
- Routine execution with no discretion needed
- Knowing maintains situational awareness
- Intervention only if exception

**Examples:**
- Calendar entry from school email
- Routine transaction completed
- Package delivered notification
- Daily metrics email
- Invoice paid automatically
- Meeting scheduled by assistant

**Design principle:** Execute silently, notify clearly, escalate only when needed.

---

## The Design Question

For any process, ask:

> What level of human involvement serves the purpose?

Not: What can we automate?  
Not: What should humans do?  
But: **What depth of presence does this deserve?**

---

## Application

### Personal Systems
Map your activities to levels. Notice:
- Are Level 1 activities protected, or crowded out?
- Are Level 4 activities consuming Level 2 attention?
- Where is drift occurring?

### Business Design
For each process:
1. Identify the level that serves the purpose
2. Design technology to support that level (not replace it)
3. Measure success by appropriate metrics
   - Level 1: Meaning, connection, identity
   - Level 2: Quality of decision, alignment with values
   - Level 3: Speed, accuracy, exception rate
   - Level 4: Silence, reliability, escalation clarity

---

## Relationship to Other Frameworks

This framework categorizes by **human depth**, not by:
- Task type (administrative vs. creative)
- Skill level (junior vs. senior)
- Automation potential (easy vs. hard)

It is compatible with but distinct from:
- RPA (Robotic Process Automation) frameworks
- Human-in-the-loop ML systems
- Time management matrices (urgent/important)
- Attention economy critiques

Research targets for comparison:
- Cal Newport's "deep work" vs. shallow work
- Tim Ferriss's "low information diet"
- Buddhist economics (right livelihood, right effort)
- Human-computer interaction (HCl) involvement taxonomies
- Workflow automation maturity models

## Summary

| Level | Depth | Role | Function | Question |
|-------|-------|------|----------|----------|
| Full Control | Deep presence | Human *is* process | Identity-forming | Who am I becoming? |
| Heavy Involvement | Significant input | Human decides | Values-expressing | What do I stand for? |
| Approval | Review/confirm | Human validates | Judgment-scaling | Is this acceptable? |
| Awareness | Notification | Human knows | Context-maintaining | What's happening? |

The framework preserves human agency by design—not as a constraint, but as an intention.
