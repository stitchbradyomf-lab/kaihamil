## The Channel's Recurring Motif

> **"Data is new event streams plus existing content.
> Outcome is decision-making capability.
> The right data depends on the question you're asking — and there are only four kinds."**

This becomes the spine of the channel. Every Pillar 1 video can return to it. Every Pillar 2 video can reframe it through System // Self. Every Pillar 3 video shows it in operation.

It's the channel's *koan*.

### The four canonical decision-questions

1. **Diagnostic** — "Is anything failing or broken?"
2. **Allocative** — "Where should I invest time/resources/attention?"
3. **Directional** — "Where is this trending, and what does it imply?"
4. **Identity / Values** — "Is this aligned with who I'm trying to become?"

Full taxonomy with examples and Stitch operating notes at `learn/decision-question-taxonomy.md`.

Every video in the queue should sharpen by asking: **which question type does this help the viewer answer?**

### #1 — "I spent 18 years inside the most data-rigorous company in the world. Here's what I learned about AI that no one is telling you."

**Pillar:** The Inside View (anchor)
**Length:** 8-10 min
**Core idea:** Three generations of technology — manual → systematic → automated → agentic. Each transition broke a category of human work AND created a new one. The current wave isn't different in kind, only in speed. What changes for you depends on whether you understand what data and decisions actually are.

**Sets up:** The channel's voice, the credential, the System // Self frame, the data motif.

**Hook line:** *"Most of what you're reading about AI is being written by people who weren't in the room for what happened with data the last three times."*

### #2 — "Data Management 101: What it actually is, and why most people get it wrong"

**Pillar:** The Inside View (motif anchor)
**Length:** 8-10 min
**Core idea:** The canonical version of Kyle's framing.

- Data = **two things**: new event streams + existing content
- Both must be captured, structured, and surfaced *at the moment of decision*
- The whole game of data management is: getting the right information in front of the right person at the right time to make a better decision
- AI changes none of this. It changes the cost of doing it well.

**Sets up:** Every subsequent video can reference "the 101." The channel's first piece of evergreen reference content.

**Hook line:** *"There are only two kinds of data: things that just happened, and things that already exist. Everything else is just storage."*

### #3 — "The Center is the position"

**Pillar:** System // Self (philosophy anchor)
**Length:** 8-10 min
**Core idea:** Why both extremes — pure-system tech maximalism AND pure-self refusal-to-engage — are failures. The Center is not neutrality. It's active commitment to synthesis. The hardest position to hold. The most valuable one to teach.

**Sets up:** KH's philosophical brand. Distinguishes Kyle from every "AI tools" creator and every "go back to the woods" creator simultaneously.

**Hook line:** *"You can't out-tech the people building AI all day. You can't out-Walden the people leaving for the woods. There's a third position — and it's the one almost no one is teaching."*

### #5 — "Three generations of data — and what's actually new this time"

**Pillar:** The Inside View
**Length:** 9-11 min
**Core idea:** A deeper version of #1, focused specifically on the data-quality trajectory.
- Gen 1: Humans doing data work directly (high error, low scale)
- Gen 2: Systematized human pipelines (medium error, medium scale)
- Gen 3: Automated extraction with human QA (low error, high scale)
- Gen 4: Agentic systems with intent-driven QA (the open question)

What's actually new isn't intelligence — it's the **collapse of the marginal cost of decision support**.

**Hook line:** *"The cost of a good decision used to be a team of analysts. Then a workflow. Now it's a prompt. That's the whole story."*

### #6 — "The two questions an AI strategy should actually answer"

**Pillar:** System // Self (operational version)
**Length:** 8-10 min
**Core idea:** Most companies ask "what can we automate?" The right questions are:
1. What do you WANT your people to do? (The Self question)
2. What information do they need to do it better? (The System question)

If your AI strategy doesn't start with those two questions, you're optimizing the wrong layer.

**Sets up:** Workshop-adjacent material without becoming a sales pitch. Reusable for any operator audience.

**Hook line:** *"There are exactly two AI strategy questions worth asking, and neither of them is 'what should we automate?'"*

### #7 — "Why the future of work is not 'do less of your job'"

**Pillar:** System // Self
**Length:** 8-10 min
**Core idea:** The dominant framing of AI at work — "let it handle the boring stuff so you can focus on the meaningful stuff" — is a comforting lie that misses the actual transition. The real shift is:
- *What counts as your job is changing*
- *The default identity 'I am a person who does X' is dissolving*
- *The replacement identity 'I am a person who decides X' is what System // Self is preparing for*

This is the philosophical companion to #5.

**Hook line:** *"Stop trying to use AI to do less of your job. The job itself is changing under you. Here's what comes next."*

## Production Notes (For When Kyle Actually Starts)

### The format constraint

8-12 minutes. Talking head + B-roll. No music intro. No "hey what's up." Begin in the middle. End with one decisive sentence.

### The visual language

Brady Visuals provides the existing aesthetic. Carry that into the channel:
- Warm/earthy color palette (matches the kaihamil-content site)
- Documentary-grade B-roll (not stock)
- On-screen text only for genuine reinforcement, never decoration
- The screen is treated as a paper, not a billboard

### The cadence

One video per week. Friday or Saturday publish. Pre-produced by Tuesday. Tuesday-Thursday for thumbnail and metadata work. **One per week is the entire game.**

### The metric

Average view duration as % of length. >50% means the expertise is landing. <30% means the script is wrong, not the camera.

### The title formula

`[Specific claim about Kyle's experience] + [Implied contrast with conventional wisdom]`

Examples already used above:
- "I spent 18 years... here's what no one is telling you"
- "What it actually is, and why most people get it wrong"
- "[Common framing] is not [what people think]"
