# Decision Framework: Path Selection Under Uncertainty

*How to choose when you can only take one path*

---

## The Problem

Many paths forward. You can only take one.

The act of choosing means giving up all others. This creates uncertainty — not knowing which future will unfold.

---

## The Nature of Uncertainty

Uncertainty comes from **not knowing what the future will be like**.

We see this as risk. Something to mitigate. So we project.

---

## The Projection

Projection creates a point in the future with characteristics:

- **Goal** — a desired state
- **Fear** — an undesired state

Both are made up. Both are mental constructs. But both shape probability.

---

## The Mechanism

### Focus on Fear
- Raises probability of feared pathways
- Increases visibility of what you don't want
- Expands the range of futures you try to avoid

### Focus on Goal
- Narrows the pathway most likely to lead there
- Increases visibility of desired outcomes
- Contracts the range of futures toward the target

---

## The Visualization

Imagine many paths radiating from where you stand.

One path lights up — not because it's objectively correct, but because it connects to a point in the future you've chosen to navigate toward.

The light isn't on the path. It's on the destination, reflected back.

---

## The Question

> Is that the point you wish to navigate towards?

This is the only question that matters.

Not: "Which path is safest?"
Not: "Which path is most likely?"

But: "What future am I lighting up?"

---

## Application

**When facing multiple options:**

1. Acknowledge uncertainty — it's real, not a failure
2. Notice your projections — are they goals or fears?
3. Ask the navigation question — what future am I choosing?
4. Accept the cost — other paths close, and that's the point

---

## The Paradox

You don't fully control which path you take.

But you shape:
- The options available (through preparation)
- The probability of each (through focus)
- The destination that lights up (through intention)
